**Goal:** Recruit a small, narrow pilot cohort — tens to low hundreds of users, not a broad launch — to test product-market fit signal before Week 3+ Monetisation. This is a plan to hand to Anshuman for review and (his) execution, not a campaign in motion.

## 1. Niche selection

Pre-PMF at this scale lives or dies on picking one narrow slice and going deep, not spreading across several. I'm proposing one primary candidate below and flagging the alternative I considered and rejected for now.

**Primary candidate: final-year B.Tech CS/IT students at 2 non-elite (non-IIT/NIT) engineering colleges, currently in an active placement cycle, who have never sat through a real interview before.**

Why this one:
- It's not invented for this exercise — the product already targets it. The landing page's tag row ("Campus placements," "First job hunt," "First technical interview") and its lead testimonial ("This was my first-ever interview, mock or real, and I had no idea what to expect") are both built around exactly this person. Recruiting this cohort tests the thing the product already claims to be for, instead of testing a new hypothesis on top of an unproven one.
- August–November is campus placement season in India for most engineering colleges — the timing lines up now, not in three months.
- "Non-elite" is the actual narrowing move here, not just "engineering student." IIT/NIT students have institutional placement prep (dedicated cells, seniors' question banks, mock-interview clubs) already partially solving this problem. Tier-2/3 college students have real placement anxiety and comparatively thin support — that's the underserved slice, and it's a better story for outreach ("we built this because your college doesn't have what IIT-Bombay's placement cell has") than a generic pitch.
- Two colleges, not five or ten. At this stage the goal is depth of feedback per user, and two gives enough volume (a section is typically 60-120 CS/IT final-years) without diluting attention across unrelated networks.

**Alternative considered, not recommended as primary:** a single Indian placement-prep subreddit (r/developersIndia, r/csMajors, or similar) or a placement-prep Discord/Telegram community, used as a *secondary* channel rather than the primary one. Reasoning below (Section 2) — treating this as a parallel, smaller test rather than the main push, because community members self-select for people who already do some prep (Discord/Reddit power users), which is a slightly different, more sophisticated population than the "never interviewed before" ICP the product is actually built for.

**Flag for Product/Anshuman:** I'm proposing this niche from a positioning/channel-fit lens — Product may have a different read based on user research I don't have visibility into (e.g. if early signal says non-CS majors, MBA freshers, or a different geography convert better). If Product's read differs, defer to it; this is my best guess, not a settled call.

## 2. Positioning / messaging for this niche

Core angle: **"Your college doesn't have a placement cell like IIT's. This is the practice you're not getting otherwise."**

This reframes the product's existing "evidence-first, not vibes" positioning (already on the landing page) for a specific gap this niche actually feels: elite-college students have seniors, mock panels, and placement-cell drilling; tier-2/3 students are often prepping alone or off YouTube. That gap is the wedge, not "AI interview coach" as a generic category claim (which invites comparison to Pramp/Final Round AI/Huru on their terms, not ours).

Secondary messages to carry through, all already validated in the existing landing copy — reuse, don't reinvent:
- "This is practice, not a real hiring decision" — lowers the stakes framing for someone anxious about even a mock.
- "Never sat through a real interview? Good, that's exactly where you start" — direct self-identification, already on the homepage hero.
- Evidence over vibes: a report backed by direct quotes from their own answers, not a generic score — this is the differentiator against "just talk to an AI" competitors and it's concrete enough to repeat verbatim in outreach.

What NOT to lead with for this niche: pricing tier names, "unlimited mocks," anything monetization-flavored. There's no live paid tier (Week 3+ Monetisation hasn't shipped), and even once it has, a free pilot cohort should be pitched as free access in exchange for feedback, not as a preview of a paid product — mixing those two asks muddies both.

## 3. Channels (small-scale, narrow, not paid/broad)

Ranked by expected trust-per-reach at this scale — direct/warm channels first, colder/public ones last.

**1. Warm network → college club coordinators (primary channel)**
Direct outreach (DM/email) to student leads of one active, relevant club per target college — GDSC, IEEE student chapter, coding club, or the training-and-placement cell's student coordinator, not the official TPO (faster, less bureaucratic than going through a college administration). Ask them to share with 15-20 final-years directly, or connect to a small group for a short intro call. This is the single highest-trust channel for a first cohort because the ask travels through someone the students already trust, not a cold link.

**2. Anshuman's own network (parallel, low-effort)**
Any 2nd-degree contacts (former classmates, colleagues, alumni) who are current students, recent grads, or know someone at a target college. Ask 5-10 people to each forward to 3-5 relevant students. Cheapest channel to activate, highest per-contact conversion, but doesn't scale past ~20-40 people — treat as a floor, not the main engine.

**3. One placement-prep community (secondary, smaller test)**
A single existing WhatsApp/Telegram/Discord group already used by students for placement-cycle coordination (not one PrepSignals creates) — requires getting an admin's explicit permission to post, not just dropping a link in. Best found through the same club coordinators in channel 1 ("is there a group your batch uses for placement stuff?") rather than cold-searching for one.

**4. One Reddit post (secondary, one-shot)**
A single post in r/developersIndia or a comparable subreddit, framed transparently as "I built this, looking for early testers," complying with that sub's self-promotion rules (check for a designated self-promo thread or required mod pre-approval before posting — do not just post cold). One post, not cross-posted across multiple subs — cross-posting reads as spam and risks a ban that closes the channel for later use.

**Explicitly deprioritized for this niche:** LinkedIn (weak engagement channel for current students vs. Discord/WhatsApp/Instagram — better fit later for the "career switcher" or working-professional segment, not this cohort), any paid promotion or influencer spend (premature pre-PMF and pre-monetization per the brief), broad content marketing / SEO (too slow for a tens-of-people, weeks-not-months pilot).

## 4. Funnel estimate (3-week draft plan)

Rough, directional — not a forecast to hold anyone to. Built from typical cold-community/warm-referral conversion patterns, not from any PrepSignals-specific data (there isn't any yet).

| Week | Activity | Est. reached | Est. clicks | Est. signups | Est. completed 1 mock interview |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify + get permission from 2 club coordinators + 1 community admin; warm-network asks go out | 40-70 (warm network) | 15-25 | 8-15 | 5-10 |
| 2 | Club coordinators share with their 15-20 each (2 colleges); community post goes live (if permission granted) | 150-250 | 30-50 | 15-25 | 8-15 |
| 3 | Reddit post; second wave to anyone who didn't convert in wk 1-2; direct follow-up with early completers for feedback | 200-400 (mostly cold, lower conversion) | 25-45 | 12-20 | 6-12 |
| **Total (3 wk)** | | **~400-700 reached** | **~70-120 clicks** | **~35-60 signups** | **~20-35 completed sessions** |

Assumptions behind the drop-off (click→signup ~40-50%, signup→completed ~55-65%): the product is free with no card required and the session is under 30 minutes, both of which should push completion higher than a typical cold-funnel benchmark — but this is a first-timer audience that may be more hesitant to actually speak into a mic for a mock interview, which could push it the other way. Treat the completed-session range as the real target metric, not raw reach.

**Stretch case:** if the club-coordinator channel overperforms (a coordinator posts to a larger year-wide group instead of just their club) or the Reddit post gets meaningful upvotes, total completed sessions could reach 60-100 in the same 3 weeks. **Downside case:** if community/coordinator permission is slow or declined, the warm-network channel alone likely caps out around 15-25 completed sessions in 3 weeks — still a usable pilot signal, just smaller.

**After week 3:** don't scale up the same channels yet. Pause, read the actual completion/feedback data, and decide whether to go deeper on the same niche (more colleges of the same profile) or pivot the niche entirely — that decision point is itself a deliverable back to Anshuman, not something to pre-decide here.
